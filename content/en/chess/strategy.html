Chess strategy is the long-term planning of a game, based on features of the position that change slowly, such as the pawn structure, the activity of the pieces and the safety of the kings.[1] It contrasts with tactics, which are short forcing sequences, and it takes over once the opening has developed the pieces.

Strategic factors

A weakness is a feature of a position, such as an undefended pawn or a weak square, that the opponent can target. Strategy consists largely of creating such weaknesses in the opponent's position and avoiding them in one's own.

Pawn structure

Pawns cannot move backwards, so their placement defines the character of the position for many moves.[2] Common terms are:

  • Passed pawn: a pawn with no enemy pawns in front of it on its own file or on the adjacent files.
  • Isolated pawn: a pawn with no friendly pawns on the adjacent files.
  • Doubled pawns: two pawns of the same colour on the same file.
  • Backward pawn: a pawn that is behind the neighbouring pawns and cannot safely advance.

Passed pawn

A passed pawn cannot be stopped by enemy pawns, so it can become a decisive advantage, especially in the endgame.[3] Its strength increases when other pieces or pawns support it.

Isolated pawn

An isolated pawn has no friendly pawns on the adjacent files, so no pawn can defend it.[4] Pieces must protect it instead, which can limit their freedom. In return, the pawn often gives its owner more space and open lines for attack.

Show the explanation

White has no pawns on the c or e files, so the pawn on d4 cannot be defended by another pawn. Black's pawns on c6 and e6 also control the square in front of it, d5. This pawn is isolated, but it is not passed, because the Black pawns on c6 and e6 can still stop it.

Open files

An open file is a file with no pawns of either side.[5] Rooks are most effective there. A rook on an open file can invade the opponent's position, for example by reaching the seventh rank, where it attacks pawns from the side.

Show the explanation

Neither side has a pawn on the e file, so it is open, and the White rook already controls it. A move such as 1. Re7 brings the rook to the seventh rank, where it attacks the pawns on d7 and f7. The rook is safe there, because no Black piece or pawn attacks e7.

Planning approach

Before choosing a plan, evaluate the position by comparing material, king safety, piece activity and pawn structure. Then pick one realistic goal, such as attacking a weakness or improving the worst-placed piece, and check that the plan does not allow a tactical shot. Studying annotated games shows how strong players connect these factors.
