Chess tactics

This article is part of the Chess category.

Chess tactics
Time horizonShort term (a few moves)
Main goalsWin material, deliver checkmate
Common motifsFork, pin, skewer
RelatedOpenings, strategy

A chess tactic is a short sequence of forcing moves that creates a concrete advantage, such as winning material or delivering checkmate.[1] A forcing move, such as a check or a capture, limits the opponent's replies. Tactics contrast with strategy, which concerns long-term planning, and they usually arise after the opening.

Fork

A fork is a single move that attacks two or more enemy pieces at the same time.[2] Knights are especially effective because their move pattern is difficult to anticipate.

Show the solution

1. Nc7+ attacks the king on e8 and the rook on a8 at the same time. The king must move, and White then captures the rook with 2. Nxa8. White wins a rook.

Pin

A pin attacks a piece that cannot move without exposing a more valuable piece behind it.[3] When the piece behind is the king, the pin is absolute: moving the pinned piece would be illegal.

Show the explanation

The bishop on b4 attacks along the diagonal to the king on e1, and the knight on d2 stands in between. The knight cannot make any legal move, because moving it would leave the king in check.

Skewer

A skewer is the reverse of a pin: the more valuable piece must move, and the piece behind it is then captured.[4]

Show the solution

1. Re1+ gives check along the e-file. The king must leave the file, and the queen on e8, which stood behind it, is then captured with 2. Rxe8.

Training approach

Tactical skill comes from pattern recognition, so short daily sessions of puzzles work better than occasional long ones. Before each move, check the forcing options in order: checks, captures and direct threats. Solving a puzzle completely in the mind before moving any piece builds the calculation habit that games demand.
