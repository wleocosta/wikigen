Financial accounting is the branch of accounting that records the transactions of an organisation and summarises them in financial statements for external users, such as investors, creditors and regulators.[1] Its rules are set by accounting standards, so that statements from different organisations can be compared.

The accounting equation

The foundation of financial accounting is the accounting equation:[2]

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

  • Asset: a resource controlled by the entity from which future economic benefits are expected, such as cash, inventory or equipment.
  • Liability: a present obligation of the entity to transfer economic resources, such as repaying a loan.
  • Equity: the residual interest in the assets after deducting liabilities.
  • Revenue: the income arising from the entity's ordinary activities, such as the sale of goods.

Transactions are recorded by double-entry bookkeeping: each transaction affects at least two accounts, and total debits equal total credits, so the equation always remains balanced.

Financial statements

The main financial statements are:[3]

  • Balance sheet (Balanço Patrimonial): a snapshot of assets, liabilities and equity at a specific date.
  • Income statement (Demonstração do Resultado do Exercício, DRE): revenues and expenses over a period, ending in profit or loss.
  • Statement of cash flows (Demonstração dos Fluxos de Caixa, DFC): the cash that entered and left the entity during the period.
  • Statement of changes in equity (Demonstração das Mutações do Patrimônio Líquido, DMPL): how equity changed during the period.
  • Notes: additional information that explains the figures in the statements.

Brazilian context

In Brazil, the Accounting Pronouncements Committee (Comitê de Pronunciamentos Contábeis, CPC) issues the technical pronouncements that guide accounting practice. CPC 26 (R1) sets the general requirements for the presentation of financial statements, and the securities regulator (CVM) makes it mandatory for listed companies.[4] A newer pronouncement, CPC 51, is based on IFRS 18 on presentation and disclosure, so the version in force for a given period should always be checked at the official source.

Study approach

Understanding the accounting equation and double-entry bookkeeping comes first, because every other topic builds on them. The balance sheet and the income statement follow, since they appear most often in examinations. Solving exercises with real figures builds speed, and each answer should be checked against the equation.
