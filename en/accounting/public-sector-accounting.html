Public sector accounting

This article is part of the Accounting category.

Public sector accounting (Brazil)

RelatedFinancial accounting, auditing

Public sector accounting is the application of accounting to government bodies, such as ministries, states, municipalities and public agencies. Its central goals are accountability, transparency and the control of public resources, rather than profit.[1] It provides budgetary, financial and equity information to support decision-making and the reporting of how public money was used.

Legal and technical framework

  • Law 4.320/1964: the basic law that sets general rules for budgets, balance sheets and public accounts.
  • Fiscal Responsibility Law (Lei de Responsabilidade Fiscal, LC 101/2000): the Fiscal Responsibility Law sets rules for the planning and control of public spending.
  • MCASP (Manual de Contabilidade Aplicada ao Setor Público): the manual issued by the National Treasury (Secretaria do Tesouro Nacional) to standardise procedures across federal, state and municipal governments.[2]
  • PCASP (Plano de Contas Aplicado ao Setor Público): the chart of accounts used to record transactions in a uniform way.
  • NBC TSP: the public sector accounting standards of the Federal Accounting Council (CFC), which pursue convergence with international public sector accounting standards (IPSAS).[3]

Budget instruments

The Brazilian Constitution establishes three budget laws that connect planning with execution:[4]

  • PPA (Plano Plurianual): the multi-year plan, which sets medium-term goals.
  • LDO (Lei de Diretrizes Orçamentárias): the budget guidelines law, which sets yearly priorities and targets.
  • LOA (Lei Orçamentária Anual): the annual budget law, which authorises revenue and expenditure for the year.

Stages of public expenditure

Public spending follows a sequence of stages, each with a specific accounting meaning.

Main stages of budgetary expenditure
StagePortuguese termMeaning
CommitmentEmpenhoThe reservation of part of a budget appropriation for a specific expense.
VerificationLiquidaçãoThe confirmation that the goods or services were delivered as contracted.
PaymentPagamentoThe disbursement of the money to the supplier.

An appropriation is the amount that the budget law authorises a body to spend on a specific purpose. A commitment cannot exceed the available appropriation.

Financial statements

The public sector equivalents of the financial statements are known as Demonstrações Contábeis Aplicadas ao Setor Público (DCASP). Law 4.320/1964 lists four main statements, and the technical standards add others, such as the cash flow statement:[2]

  • Budget statement (Balanço Orçamentário): compares the forecast and the execution of revenue and expenditure.
  • Financial statement (Balanço Financeiro): shows receipts, payments and cash balances.
  • Balance sheet (Balanço Patrimonial): shows assets, liabilities and equity.
  • Statement of equity variations (Demonstração das Variações Patrimoniais): shows changes in equity caused by public activity.

Study approach

Learn the legal framework first, because exam questions often refer to a specific law or standard. Then memorise the order of the expenditure stages and the purpose of each budget law and statement. Because technical standards and manuals are updated, always check the edition adopted by the exam syllabus.
