Auditing is the independent examination of an organisation's records, processes or financial statements to verify that they are accurate and comply with applicable rules.[1] The best known form is the audit of financial statements, which ends with a report in which the auditor expresses an opinion on whether the statements present a fair view.[2]

Key concepts

  • Independence: independence from the audited entity is the foundation of the profession, because an opinion is only credible if the auditor is free from influence.
  • Materiality: materiality is the threshold above which an error or omission could influence the decisions of users of the statements.
  • Professional scepticism: professional scepticism means questioning information critically instead of accepting it at face value.
  • Audit evidence: the auditor gathers sufficient and appropriate evidence, such as documents, confirmations from third parties and recalculations, to support the opinion.

The audit process

  1. Planning: understanding the entity and assessing the risk of material misstatement.
  2. Testing: designing and performing procedures to obtain evidence.
  3. Evaluation: comparing the errors found with the materiality threshold.
  4. Reporting: issuing the audit report with the opinion.

Audit opinions

The auditor's report usually takes one of four forms, according to international auditing standards:[3]

  • Unmodified (clean) opinion: the statements present a fair view.
  • Qualified opinion: the statements are fair except for a specific matter.
  • Adverse opinion: the statements are materially misstated.
  • Disclaimer of opinion: the auditor could not obtain enough evidence to form an opinion.

Brazilian public sector context

In Brazil, public spending is subject to internal and external control. The Federal Court of Accounts (Tribunal de Contas da União, TCU) supports the National Congress in the external control of federal accounts,[4] while the Office of the Comptroller General (Controladoria-Geral da União, CGU) performs internal control and audit functions in the federal executive branch.[5] Professional audit standards are issued in Brazil by the Federal Accounting Council (Conselho Federal de Contabilidade, CFC) in convergence with the international standards. Since the exact scope and rules of each body are set by law and can change, the official sources should be consulted for exam preparation.

Study approach

Start with the key concepts and the four opinions, because they are stable and frequently examined. Then link each concept to the stage of the process in which it applies. Past exam questions show how each concept is tested.
