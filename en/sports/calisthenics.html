Calisthenics

Calisthenics is a form of strength training that uses the practitioner's own body weight as resistance.[1] The name comes from the Greek words kallos, meaning beauty, and sthenos, meaning strength. Because it needs little or no equipment, calisthenics is a low-cost way to train at home or outdoors, and it combines well with other activities such as jump rope.

Basic exercises

Five movements cover most of the body and form the basis of many routines. Each has easier and harder variations, so the same exercise can suit a beginner or an advanced practitioner.

Progression

Progression is the gradual increase of difficulty, so that the body keeps adapting to the effort.[7] Body weight cannot be added in small steps as with weights, so difficulty is changed through leverage, range of motion, tempo and exercise variation. A repetition is a single completed movement, and a set is a group of consecutive repetitions. A practical sequence is:

  1. Learn the movement with an easier variation and correct form.
  2. Increase the repetitions and sets until the target is reached comfortably.
  3. Move to a harder variation and reduce the repetitions again.
  4. Slow the tempo or add pauses to increase the difficulty without changing the exercise.

Training approach

Regular sessions with rest days between them allow the muscles to recover. A short warm-up reduces the risk of injury, and correct technique matters more than the number of repetitions. Recording each session, in a notebook or with a sensor or wearable device, makes progress measurable and shows when a variation is ready to change.

This article is educational and does not constitute medical advice. People with health conditions should consult a professional before starting a new training programme.
