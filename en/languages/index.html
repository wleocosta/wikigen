Language learning is the process of acquiring the ability to understand and use a language other than one's first. In this encyclopedia, the subject refers to the author's study of languages, which begins with English and extends to other foreign languages.

Overview

The author's first language is Portuguese, and this encyclopedia is written in English first. English serves as a common language for technical documentation, international finance and online communities, which makes fluency in it a practical priority.

A single term can look very different across languages. The Portuguese word biblioteca, for example, resembles the Spanish biblioteca but not the English library, a difference caused by distinct historical roots.

Areas of study

  • Vocabulary: the words and expressions needed to understand and communicate.
  • Grammar: the rules that govern how words combine into sentences.
  • Listening and speaking: the understanding of spoken language and the production of clear speech.
  • Reading and writing: the comprehension of texts and the composition of clear, accurate prose.

Study approach

A relatively small set of high-frequency words accounts for a large share of everyday text, so these words are learned first. Regular review at increasing intervals, known as spaced repetition, strengthens long-term memory. Daily exposure through reading, listening and writing builds fluency more reliably than occasional long sessions.
