Variable income is a group of investments whose returns are not defined in advance and depend on market prices, company results and economic conditions.[1] It contrasts with fixed income, in which the rules for remuneration are set at purchase or linked to an index.

Stocks

A stock is a unit of ownership in a company.[2] Buying a stock makes the investor a shareholder, a part-owner of the company, with rights that depend on the type of share. In Brazil, common shares usually carry voting rights, while preferred shares usually carry priority in the distribution of dividends. Stocks are traded on an exchange, where prices change continuously according to supply and demand.

Dividends

A dividend is a portion of a company's profit distributed to its shareholders. Dividends are decided by the company and are not guaranteed: a company may reduce or suspend them when its results decline or when it needs to retain cash.

Futures

A futures contract is an agreement to buy or sell an asset at a set price on a future date.[3] Futures are leveraged instruments: a small deposit, called margin, controls a much larger position, so losses can exceed the amount deposited. For this reason, they are generally regarded as suitable only for investors who understand their mechanics and risks.

Valuation indicators

Valuation is the process of estimating what a company or share is worth. Two common indicators are the price-to-earnings ratio (share price divided by earnings per share) and the dividend yield (dividends per share divided by share price). The data comes from the company's financial statements, which links this subject to accounting.

Risks

  • Market risk: prices can fall because of general economic or political events. Volatility, the degree to which a price rises and falls over time, is a central feature of variable income markets.
  • Company risk: the results of a specific company can disappoint.
  • Leverage risk: borrowed or margin-based positions magnify both gains and losses.
  • Liquidity risk: some assets are difficult to sell quickly without a loss in value.
