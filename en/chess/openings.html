A chess opening is the sequence of moves played at the start of a game, before the position becomes too complex to follow established patterns.[1] A sound opening prepares the pieces for the middlegame, where tactics and strategy decide the result.

Principles

Most openings follow the same general principles: control the centre, develop the pieces, castle early and avoid moving the same piece twice. Castling, a special move that tucks the king away and activates a rook, is usually played within the first ten moves. Early queen moves are generally avoided, because the queen can be attacked by lesser pieces and the player loses time.

Notation

Algebraic notation records each move with the letter of the piece and the square it moves to.[2] The letters are K (king), Q (queen), R (rook), B (bishop) and N (knight); pawn moves have no letter. For example, 1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 means that White plays a pawn to e4, Black answers with a pawn to e5, and White develops a knight to f3.

Classification

Openings are classified by their first moves into open, semi-open and closed games. The Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings assigns each variation a code from A00 to E99.[3]

Study approach

Understanding the ideas behind the moves is more useful than memorising long variations. A practical plan is:

  1. Learn the general principles above and apply them in every game.
  2. Choose one opening as White and one reply to each of 1. e4 and 1. d4 as Black.
  3. Review each completed game to find where the opening plan failed.
  4. Expand the repertoire only when these first choices feel comfortable.
