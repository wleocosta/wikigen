Automation is the use of technology to perform tasks with minimal human intervention.[1] In this encyclopedia, the subject refers to home and industrial automation, which combine three fields covered in this category: electronics for the hardware, programming for the logic, and control, the method by which a system corrects its own behaviour.

Control loop

Most automated systems repeat a cycle of three steps: sense, decide and act.[2]

The three stages of a control loop
StageComponentRoleExample
SenseSensor[3]Measures a physical quantityA temperature sensor
DecideControllerCompares the measurement with a target and chooses an actionA microcontroller or a programmable logic controller[4]
ActActuator[5]Changes something in the physical worldA relay that switches a heater or a motor

A thermostat is the simplest example: a sensor measures the room temperature, the controller compares it with the target, and the actuator switches the heater on or off. When the result of an action is measured again and used to correct the next action, the system uses feedback. A system without feedback, such as a timer that switches a light on at a fixed hour, is called open-loop.

Safety

Automated systems act on the physical world, so a fault can cause damage or injury. Safe practice includes:

  • Low voltage first: practise with batteries and 5 V supplies, as in electronics.
  • Mains circuits: lighting, heaters and sockets use household voltage, and their installation should be done only by qualified people.
  • Safe state: the system is designed so that a failure of a sensor, a program or the power leaves it in a safe condition.
  • Manual override: a switch returns control to a person at any moment.
  • Industrial machinery: moving machines can injure people, so safety rules and protective devices are required.

This article is educational and is not a substitute for professional training.

Study approach

A small complete loop teaches more than a large unfinished system, so a practical sequence is:

  1. Learn the basics of electronics, including voltage, current and resistance.
  2. Build a small sense, decide and act project, such as a sensor that switches an LED or a relay.
  3. Write the controller's program with simple rules, such as switching a fan on when the temperature is above the target.
  4. Add feedback, a manual override and a safe state, and only then extend the system to larger devices.
