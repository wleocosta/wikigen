Robotics

Robotics is the field concerned with the design, construction and programming of robots, which are machines that sense their environment and act on it.[1][2] In this encyclopedia, the subject refers to hands-on robotics, which combines mechanics, electronics and programming. It extends automation to machines that move, either as a whole or through arms and tools.

Main parts

Main parts of a robot
PartRoleExample
SensorsMeasure the environment or the state of the robot itselfA distance sensor, a camera or a rotary encoder[3]
ControllerRuns the program that decides what to doA microcontroller or a small computer
ActuatorsProduce movementA servo motor[4] or a stepper motor[5]
Power supplyProvides electrical energyA battery pack
StructureHolds the parts together and transmits movementA frame, wheels, joints and gears

A robot repeats the same sense, decide and act cycle as any control loop in automation, but its actuators move the machine itself or its tools. A robot that measures the rotation of its wheels with an encoder and corrects its speed is using feedback.

Safety

A robot is a machine with moving parts and an energy source, so building one involves risks. Safe practice includes:

  • Moving parts: motors, gears and wheels can pinch fingers or cause injury, so hands and clothing are kept clear while the robot is powered.
  • Power off: the battery is disconnected before the wiring is changed.
  • Batteries: lithium batteries can overheat or ignite if they are short-circuited or overcharged, so only the matching charger is used.
  • Safe testing: a new program is first tested with the wheels lifted off the ground, so that an error cannot send the robot across the table.
  • Larger robots: stronger motors can cause serious injury, so a safe distance and an emergency stop are required.

This article is educational and is not a substitute for professional training.

Study approach

A small working robot teaches more than a complex unfinished one, so a practical sequence is:

  1. Learn the basics of electronics and programming.
  2. Build a small wheeled robot with two motors, a controller and a distance sensor.
  3. Write a program with simple rules, such as turning away when the sensor detects an obstacle closer than a set distance.
  4. Add feedback, such as wheel encoders, and refine the behaviour step by step.
